# Trigger Protocol Specification

Version: 0.3
Status: Experimental

## 1. Purpose

Trigger Protocol defines a minimal, vendor-neutral semantic boundary between machine intelligence and consequential action.

The foundational rule is: **intelligence is not authority; recommendation is not authorization; authorization is not execution.**

The protocol is deliberately not a replacement for organizational governance, law, identity infrastructure, or model safety. It defines the portable records and invariants needed to keep authority explicit.

## 2. Canonical lifecycle

PROPOSE -> REVIEW -> DECIDE -> TRIGGER -> EXECUTE -> OUTCOME

A proposal may be generated by an AI system. Review may be human, organizational, or automated. Authorization is a decision made under authority. A Trigger is the explicit machine-readable authorization event. Execution is a separate event performed by an executor. Outcome records consequences.

## 3. Core primitives

The canonical vocabulary is defined in protocol/vocabulary.md. The minimum semantic objects are Actor, Agent, Action, Resource, Proposal, Review, Decision, Authority, Delegation, Trigger, Receipt, Execution, Outcome, Evidence, Constraint, Policy, and Revocation.

## 4. Authority

Authority MUST be explicit, scoped, and time-bounded where applicable. It MUST identify the holder, permitted action/domain, limits, governing policy, validity, and revocation state when those concepts exist in the deployment.

Possession of a model, tool, API credential, or execution capability does not by itself establish protocol authority.

## 5. Decision

Decision values are approve, reject, modify, defer, and request_second_opinion. Only approve can lead to a Trigger. A modification creates a new effective action representation and MUST remain linked to the original proposal.

Dissent and second opinions are durable records. They do not disappear because an approval was later issued.

## 6. Trigger

A Trigger MUST reference a proposal's decision, actor, authority, action, and issuance time. It SHOULD include resource/scope, constraints, policy version, delegation, and expiry where applicable.

A Trigger Receipt is the portable representation of this authorization event. See protocol/signature-profile.md for the optional Ed25519 signature profile.

## 7. Execution gate

An executor MUST independently verify before a consequential action:

- the referenced decision is an approval;
- the actor holds or validly derives the referenced authority;
- authority covers the action and resource/scope;
- delegation, if any, is valid and not revoked;
- the trigger is within its validity interval;
- constraints are satisfied;
- the proposal/decision/trigger references are intact.

An executor MUST reject or block execution when these checks fail. Successful execution does not retroactively legitimize a failed authorization check.

## 8. Receipt signatures\n\nThe v0.3 signature profile defines detached Ed25519 signatures over the canonical receipt representation. Signature verification authenticates receipt integrity but does not establish authority; executors must still validate authority independently.\n\n## 9. Risk and reversibility

Implementations SHOULD classify risk as low, medium, high, or critical and reversibility as reversible, partial, or irreversible. Higher impact and lower reversibility should normally require stronger authorization. The protocol does not prescribe a universal risk policy.

## 10. Audit and outcome

Execution records MUST retain the Trigger ID. Outcome records SHOULD retain the Execution ID. Audit data should be sufficient to reconstruct authority and causality while minimizing unnecessary personal data.

## 11. Versioning and interoperability

JSON/UTF-8 and JSON Schema Draft 2020-12 are the reference representation. Transport is intentionally unspecified. See protocol/interoperability.md.

Implementations MUST NOT silently reinterpret another protocol version. Unknown extension fields MUST be preserved when forwarding records.
